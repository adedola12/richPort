05 — NIQS Page Strategy

How to Present the NIQS Project

Full strategic discussion from this session — both the recruiter lens and the senior UX designer lens. This is the brief for restructuring the page once screenshots are available.

The core tension: The NIQS page currently has strong copy and structure but zero visual evidence. No recruiter will read past the hero without seeing the work. Screenshots from the HTML guideline file are the unlock. Everything else here is the strategy for what goes around them.

The 5 questions a recruiter and a senior UX designer are both asking — in order

#QuestionCurrent answer on the pageGap
1 Who is this client and why does it matter? Answered — 55 years, 10K+ members, 37 chapters Good. Keep it.
2 What was the actual problem you were solving? Answered but buried — "no unified visual language across 37 chapters" This needs to be the first thing someone reads. Move it above the stats.
3 What decision did YOU make, and why? Partially answered — constraints listed but the key decision (preserve the eagle) is just a bullet point Needs its own named section. This is the most senior signal on the page.
4 What does the work look like? Not answered — no images except logo SVGs Critical gap. Screenshots needed before the page can work.
5 What was the result? Answered — 60 pages, 12 chapters, stationery + digital applications Good. The "in production" framing needs tightening.

5 specific changes that would make the biggest difference

Change 01 — Most Important

Lead with the design tension, not the facts

The brief section currently opens with deliverable tags and role labels. The most interesting sentence on the page — "A 55-year institution. No unified visual language." — is buried in the challenge section. That needs to be the first thing someone reads, in large type, before any stats or tags. It tells you immediately that this is a real problem with real stakes.

Change 02 — Highest Signal for Senior Roles

Make "What We Didn't Change" its own named section

The decision to preserve the heraldic eagle is currently just a bullet point in a constraints list. It deserves its own section — call it "The First Decision" or "What We Didn't Change." Show the eagle. Explain explicitly: we were given a brief to refresh the identity and the first thing we decided was to leave the mark alone. That is brand strategy, not just design execution. Senior designers and creative directors will stop scrolling when they read that. It distinguishes the work from the 99% of brand projects that are just "redesign everything."

Change 03 — Credibility Fix

Replace "~60% complete" with "In Production" framing

The current status note says the guidelines are "approximately 60% complete." That is a liability. It plants doubt before any work is seen. The correct framing: "The core system — logo, colour, typography, stationery, digital guidelines — is complete and delivered. Social media templates across 37 chapters are currently in production." Same information. Completely different impression. Active work reads as professional momentum, not incompleteness.

Change 04 — Highest Visual Impact

Add at least 3 screenshots from the HTML guideline

Open extra/NIQS Brand Guideline.html in Chrome. Take full-page or section screenshots of: the cover spread, the colour palette page, and the typography specimen page. Upload to Cloudinary. Even screenshots of a well-designed HTML file read as polished portfolio content. Without any images of the actual work, no amount of good copy will save the page. A recruiter makes a visual judgment in 8 seconds. Right now they see logo SVGs only.

Change 05 — Most Memorable Detail

Move the QS connection to the opening

The closing quote currently contains the most distinctive sentence in the entire portfolio: Richard is a trained Quantity Surveyor designing FOR the professional body of Quantity Surveyors in Nigeria. That needs to be near the top of the page — not the bottom. A version of this line near the opening: "I'm a trained Quantity Surveyor. NIQS is the professional body for Quantity Surveyors in Nigeria. This wasn't a client — this was my profession." That line, read early, changes how everything after it is interpreted. It makes the design decisions more credible, the constraints more understandable, and the work impossible to forget.

What the page says right now vs. what it should say:
Right now: "Here is a brand identity project I did for a big institution."
After restructure: "Here is the institution I belong to, the brief I was given, the one decision that defined everything, and the system I built around it."
The second version earns attention. The first loses it.
